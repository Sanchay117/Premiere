# Premiere

**Paste a URL, get a launch trailer.** An AI agent opens your app, clicks through its main flow, writes a script, records real footage, and returns a 45-second narrated trailer with captions, music and thumbnails in a few minutes.

This is our entry for **Vibestarter Vibeathon 02: Night shift**. This repo will be public, so keep secrets, emails and private notes out of this file.

The build window closes **9 Nov 2026, 22:00 UTC (10 Nov, 03:30 IST)**. Aim to submit by 8 Nov.

## Product

**User flow:** paste a URL, watch live progress (opening the app, planning, recording, narrating, rendering), then download or share the trailer.

**Each trailer delivers:**
- `trailer.mp4`: 1920×1080 (1080×1920 for the vertical cut), about 45 seconds (or 30 or 15), H.264/AAC, captions burned in, loudness normalized to -16 LUFS
- `thumbnail-1920.png` and `thumbnail-1280.png`
- `captions.srt`

**Each order chooses:** the cut (`hype`: punch titles and footage on a floating screen; `walkthrough`: a calm tour), the narrator (`auto` or one of eight Aura-2 voices, sampled in `web/public/voices`), the caption look (`karaoke`, `boxed`, `bold`, `off`; the SRT always ships), the running time (`45`, `30` or `15` seconds, called Trailer, Teaser and Spot), the screen (`landscape` 16:9, or `vertical` 9:16 for TikTok, Reels and Shorts), and whether it may appear in the gallery. Two optional extras: director's notes (one line of at most 280 characters on what to show, click or type) and a signed-in demo link for apps behind a login (see the capture step). Defaults: hype, auto, karaoke, 45, landscape. Claude Opus 5.5 writes every script; there is no model choice on the site.

**Recuts:** in the screening room a finished trailer can be recut. Change any line or punch title, the narrator or the caption look, and the worker voices the edited script and cuts it over the first take's footage: no capture, no Claude call, no filming, about 30 s. The cut, running time and screen stay the first take's, since the footage was filmed for them. A recut counts as one of the visitor's free trailers for the day (and stays free once payments are on), and it never hangs in the gallery, because Claude judged the first take's lines, not the edited ones.

**Who pays what:**
- **Everyone, Vibeathon entrants included:** free until Vibeathon 02 ends (judging and the holder vote close 16 Nov), within the daily limits. That keeps the application's promise that entrants get theirs free. Entrant codes still exist as an emergency lever (`ACCESS_CODES`); the form asks for one only if the worker says so.
- **After the Vibeathon:** a few USDC per trailer (`PRICE_USDC`, default 3) on Base, paid from the visitor's wallet over x402. Turn on with `PAYMENTS=mainnet` (or `testnet` for Base Sepolia) and check with `npm run payments`. Receiving address: `PAYMENT_ADDRESS` in `.env`.
- **AI agents:** one x402 request, `POST /api/x402/trailers`, returns a job id (free during the Vibeathon); those trailers stay out of the gallery unless the order sends `gallery: true`. Coding agents can use the MCP server in `mcp/` instead, which calls the same route. Default x402 clients refuse payments over $1 unless the agent raises its cap, so consider pricing agent orders at $1 or saying so in the docs.

**Landing page:** a real trailer plays above the fold, so a judge sees the output within 2 seconds: Premiere's hype trailer of squoosh.app, narrated by Pandora, in which the agent picks a sample photo and films the side-by-side editor it opens (`web/public/sample`, 720p, 3.4 MB, muted on a loop with a sound button; on phones the screen comes before the order form). It replaced a trailer of Fundable with a male narrator; Uniswap and Linear were the other candidates. Swap in Premiere's own trailer once deployed. Below it, up to 12 lobby cards from the gallery of trailers made for other apps, hung by whoever ordered them (the site no longer claims the app's maker agreed, since anyone can order a trailer of any public app; `npm run gallery -- remove <id>` handles takedown requests).

## Architecture

```
web (site + API)  ->  job queue  ->  worker  ->  storage  ->  web shows the result
```

- **Web:** Next.js on Vercel. Landing page, job form, job status page, payment and x402 routes. Built in `web/` (Next.js 16, Tailwind v4, Motion, Lenis). Its `/api/jobs` routes proxy to the worker server-side (`WORKER_URL`, `WORKER_TOKEN`), so the browser never talks to the worker.
- **Queue and storage:** for now the worker runs its own HTTP job server (`worker/src/server`) with an in-memory queue and serves files from disk. It runs one job at a time unless `JOB_CONCURRENCY` says more (at most 4); two jobs for the same page share one capture, and the spend ledger is one shared instance so parallel plans cannot overwrite each other's cost. Measured on the 16-thread dev laptop (7 Nov): two jobs at once finished both trailers in 122 s against about 230 s one after the other, but the two shoots, running at the same moment, captured 18 to 30% fewer frames (16.2 against 19.7 fps for Corvo Edge, 11.3 against 16.1 for Squoosh). So jobs take turns filming (`inFilmingSlot` in `record/recorder.ts`; one that waits says so in its progress): with `JOB_CONCURRENCY` 2, one job can capture, plan, voice or render while another films, which keeps most of the gain without the dropped frames. A recut is a job of its own (`recutOf` names the first take) that starts at the voice step. Each job that ends is saved as `job.json` in its folder, so its page and gallery entry survive restarts. Job files go out with an ETag and `no-cache` (the web proxy forwards `If-None-Match` and `If-Range`), so a re-rendered thumbnail shows up at once while unchanged files come back as a quick 304. That is the simplest thing that works locally; before judging week decide whether it stays (worker on the VPS behind HTTPS with `WORKER_TOKEN`) or moves to a hosted database and object storage, and note the choice here.
- **Gallery:** `worker/src/server/gallery.ts`. A finished trailer goes up by itself when its maker ticked the box and Claude, in the same planning call, judged the page fit for a public wall (`publicSafe` in the plan: false for adult, hateful, violent or gory content, illegal goods, phishing or scams). Fallback plans and older jobs have no such judgment, so they are held until `npm run gallery -- approve <id>`; `npm run gallery -- remove <id>` takes any trailer down for good. Only the newest listed trailer of each app (host plus path) hangs, and the list refreshes as soon as a job finishes. This matters because the worker films any URL someone pastes.
- **Payments:** `worker/src/payments` (x402 v2 through `@x402/core` and `@x402/evm`). The worker verifies and settles through a facilitator (x402.org for Base Sepolia, PayAI for Base) before a paid job starts, and never holds a key. The site's checkout (`web/src/lib/checkout.ts`) signs in the visitor's wallet and loads only when payments are on.
- **MCP server:** `mcp/`, a stdio server that gives coding agents (Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Cursor) `make_trailer`, `trailer_status` and `premiere_options`. It calls the site's public API at `PREMIERE_URL` (the x402 order route and the job views), so it holds no keys and never pays; it always sends `gallery` (false unless the user wants it public) and never repeats a demo link. Start it with `node mcp/src/index.ts` (Node 22.18 or later runs the TypeScript itself) or `npx -y tsx mcp/src/index.ts`, never with `npm run`, whose banner on stdout breaks stdio clients. Setup for each client: `mcp/README.md`.
- **Worker:** a Node process with Playwright, Remotion and ffmpeg. It cannot run on Vercel. Run it on the dev machine during development and on a small VPS for judging week (9 to 16 Nov), so it doesn't depend on a laptop staying awake.

### Repo layout and commands

npm workspaces, Node 22 or later (developed on Node 26). Root scripts forward to the worker.

- `worker/src/capture`: Playwright capture. In-page scripts live in `capture/inpage/*.js` as plain JS, sent to the page as text (the TypeScript loader would otherwise inject helpers that do not exist in the page).
- `worker/src/planner`: schema, prompt, Claude call, fallback, validation, plan cache, mock plans, pricing and spend ledger.
- `worker/src/safety`: URL checks, egress proxy, hardened browser launch. Reuse `launchSafeBrowser` for every browser that opens a submitted URL, including the recorder.
- `worker/.data` (gitignored): captures, plan cache, spend ledger, bake-off reports, job folders and the Kokoro model (`models`). `worker/fixtures/plans` (committed): mock plans.

### The pipeline (one job)

1. **Capture (no AI).** Playwright opens the URL at 1920×1080 and collects the title, meta description, headings, every clickable element (with a stable id), and 3 or 4 screenshots downscaled to about 1024 px wide. Dismiss cookie banners. If the app is behind a login, stay on the public pages, unless the order brings a demo link (a magic link, or a URL with a demo token, that opens the app signed in to a demo account). Then `captureDemo` opens that link instead: the capture keeps the public URL and strips the query and fragment from where it landed, the plan is never cached, and the signed-in browser state (`storageState`) goes to the recorder in memory, so a single-use link is opened only once. The link gets the same URL checks as any other, is never echoed, logged or saved (the job view and `job.json` leave it out), and the trailer never hangs in the gallery. Many apps boot, sit quiet, then fetch and draw their real content several seconds in (Exdiv's market cards land at 6.5 s), so the page gets at least 7 s from navigation and then one quiet second (`waitForContent`, shared with the recorder) before it is mapped. Pages that keep their main thread busy make every Playwright round trip wait its turn, so the cookie sweep (`inpage/find-consent.js`), the scroll-through and the stability check are each one in-page call, and screenshots come straight from DevTools (`Page.captureScreenshot`) rather than Playwright's screenshot, which makes several round trips first. A bot check that came with an error status or left almost nothing on the page stops the job with a `bot_wall` capture error: Product Hunt's Cloudflare page was once planned and filmed as if it were the app.
2. **Plan (one AI call).** Claude returns a JSON shot list: ordered actions (click, scroll, type, hover, wait) that target element ids, with one narration line and one punch title (1 to 4 words) per shot, a title card, a closing call to action and the gallery judgment, written for the chosen cut and running time (`CUTS` in `planner/prompt.ts`, `WORDS_TARGETS` in `planner/schema.ts`): at 45 s, 55 to 85 words in 5 or 6 shots for hype and 100 to 125 in 6 or 7 for walkthrough; 3 or 4 shots at 30 s, 2 at 15 s. Director's notes reach Claude in a `<directors_notes>` block (control characters and angle brackets removed, so they cannot close it), and only then does the system prompt add its rules for notes: follow them where the capture allows, but they never change the rules (no invented features, no sign-ins or purchases, the same shot and word limits, `publicSafe` judged from the page alone). Notes can only ask for the five actions: "draw a rectangle" on Excalidraw became picking the rectangle tool, since there is no drag. Validate the output against the schema; if it fails, use the fallback planner.
3. **Voice** (runs before recording, so each shot is filmed for exactly as long as its line). Meanwhile the recorder's browser opens the app (`openStage`), so its launch, page load and the app's own boot are off the clock. Deepgram Aura-2, one request per line, five lines at a time (the pay-as-you-go limit is 15 concurrent Aura-2 requests per project; 429s and 5xx are retried with backoff), in the narrator the person picked (`worker/src/options.ts` lists the eight), or with `auto` one that suits the plan's mood (Atlas for energetic and bold, Asteria for playful, Athena for calm). Aura returns no timestamps, so each line is transcribed with Deepgram `nova-3` and the words are aligned back onto the script (`voice/deepgram.ts`, `alignWords`). Key: `DEEPGRAM_KEY` in `.env`. If Deepgram fails (an outage, a spent account, no key), Kokoro reads the script on the worker's CPU (`voice/kokoro.ts`: kokoro-js with its q8 model, 92 MB, downloaded into `.data/models` on first use). Each Aura narrator maps to a Kokoro voice of the same kind (Atlas to Michael, Pandora to Emma, and so on), silence is trimmed from each line, the words are spread over the speech by length (Kokoro gives no timings), and the screening room names the backup voice. `KOKORO=off` skips it. If Kokoro fails too, the lines become captions held for reading time (about 2.5 words a second, `silentLines`), the music is not ducked, captions cannot be switched off, and the screening room says so: the job still returns a trailer.
4. **Record (no AI).** `record/recorder.ts` replays the shot list in the hardened browser. Frames come from the Chrome DevTools screencast (JPEG, timestamped); the screencast sends nothing while a page is static, so a real screenshot is taken as a keyframe at the start of every shot. Each shot: setup off camera (find the element again, scroll it into view), a 0.7 s lead-in, the action, then a hold for the narration. A scroll to a section parks its heading about a fifth of the way down the screen, clear of sticky headers (parking it at the very top put the spotlight on Exdiv's nav bar). Clicks fall back to a real mouse event at the element's center when Playwright's actionability checks refuse an overlaid element. A target that is not on the page when its shot comes up gets a second look 0.7 s later, then `reveal()` opens the page's closed menu triggers one at a time (`aria-haspopup`, `aria-expanded="false"`, a closed `<details>`; at most four, never one that reads like a login or a destructive action), keeping the first that brings the target into view: Excalidraw's "Live collaboration" sits on the welcome screen at capture time and in the main menu once earlier shots pick tools. Shoots take turns (`inFilmingSlot`). A failed step is skipped in the edit, never faked; the recording has a hard time cap. A dialog, menu or listbox a click left open is closed with Escape during the next shot's setup, off camera (Uniswap's token picker once covered four shots); nothing later can target inside it, since the page was mapped with it closed. If not one planned shot could be filmed (usually because the page changed after it was mapped), the job reshoots with the no-AI planner's shots (holds and scrolls, which almost never fail) under the same name, tagline, hook, close and gallery judgment, re-voiced, and the screening room says so.
5. **Music and sound.** `audio/music.ts` synthesizes the score per mood (tempo, chords, drums), plus a whoosh on the title, ticks on clicks, and a riser and impact into the end card. A hype cut runs the groove at 124 bpm or more, drops it out under each punch title and lands a swell and an impact on every title. `audio/mix.ts` ducks it under the narration; ffmpeg `loudnorm` (two pass) masters the soundtrack to -16 LUFS before rendering.
6. **Render.** `render/timeline.ts` times the edit from the voice. A hype cut adds a 1 s punch title before each shot and a 0.4 s beat after each line; `pacing()` trims both evenly (down to 0.75 s of punch and a 0.2 s pause after each line) when the cut would pass 46 s, and the recorder films to the same pacing. A cut that would come in under 41 s holds each shot longer after its line instead (up to 1.6 s more for a walkthrough, 0.7 s for hype): on real apps Opus at low effort wrote walkthroughs of about 90 words when asked for 110, and those cut to 33 s. App names are sized by each letter's estimated width in the display face (`remotion/fit.ts`), never broken mid-word; a flat average per letter once broke "TLDRAW" into "TLDRA" and "W". The video is encoded in BT.709 at standard range (`colorSpace: "bt709"`); Remotion 4's default tagged it full-range BT.601, which some players show washed out. Remotion (`render/remotion/`) renders the title card, each filmed shot with a virtual camera that eases toward the target, an amber spotlight on the thing being clicked, hovered or typed into (never on a scroll, and only when the target sits wholly in frame and is smaller than most of the screen), a drawn cursor and click ripple (headless Chrome records no pointer), word-timed captions in the chosen look, and the end card with the app's URL and "Made with Premiere". Hype: the app name slams in (League Gothic), each punch title rises on a dark screen, and the footage plays inside a browser window that flies in tilted and settles. Walkthrough: the serif title card, full-frame shots with a quiet chapter label. A `<Still>` renders both thumbnails, and each shows the app itself in the app's own color, so no two apps get the same poster: `render/thumbnail.ts` takes the first filmed shot's frame (usually the hero; a later frame only when it is far more colorful, and blank frames never) and the strongest color in it, lifted to read on a dark poster, as the accent. Hype: the gothic title beside the app in the tilted browser window, over a blur of the same frame. Walkthrough: the app full frame, sharp up top, with the serif title along the bottom. The first version dimmed the footage almost to black behind one fixed layout, and every thumbnail looked alike. The same accent colors the trailer itself (an `Accent` context in `Trailer.tsx`): titles, punch cards, chapter labels, caption highlights and the end card, while the spotlight and click ripple stay amber. The vertical cut (1080×1920, sized by `calculateMetadata` in `Root.tsx`) carries the 16:9 footage as a card across the middle of the frame (`VerticalStage`), zoomed a little further toward each target, with the app's name and address above it and the captions below; title and end cards are the same scenes, sized to the frame's width. Each running time keeps to its own window (`RUNTIME`: 41 to 46 s for 45, 27 to 31 for 30, 13 to 16 for 15). A recut (`recutTrailer`) voices the edited plan and cuts it over the first take's `recording.json` and frames, hard-linked rather than copied; a line longer than its shot's footage holds the last frame (`freeze` in `buildTimeline`, `footageEnd` in the render). Output: `trailer.mp4` (H.264 CRF 18, x264 `veryfast`, AAC 192k), `thumbnail-1920.png`, `thumbnail-1280.png`, `captions.srt`, plus `edit.json`. Speed (benchmarked 6 Nov on the 16-thread dev laptop): one Chrome tops out around 25 to 30 frames a second whatever the concurrency (4, 8, 12 and 16 all landed there), the GPU backend (`gl: "angle"`) gains only 10% and software `swangle` is 7x slower, and the hype look costs at most 20% over a plain walkthrough. So the render shares one browser for the video and both thumbnails (rendered alongside the video), uses `veryfast` (10% quicker, slightly smaller files), and the worker bundles the compositions when it boots. Two Chrome instances in parallel reach about 36 fps together, so chunked rendering would only pay on a machine with many cores. Frames rendered scale with trailer length, which is one more reason to hold cuts near 45 s. Remotion reuses Playwright's Chrome Headless Shell, and its compositor package ships the ffmpeg the worker uses, so there is no separate ffmpeg install. Remotion is free for individuals and companies of up to three people; check the license before Premiere grows past that.

### Planner rules (Claude API)

- Model: **`claude-opus-5-5` at `effort: "low"`** for every trailer (override with `PLANNER_MODEL` on the worker; the site offers no choice). Read the claude-api skill before changing this code.
- Why Opus (bake-off on 6 Nov, 5 apps, one run each, $0.23 total): every model produced usable plans. Opus 5.5 was tightest on length and picked slightly better actions, at about twice Sonnet's cost; we chose it after first defaulting to Sonnet. Sonnet 5.5 wrote vivid, screenshot-grounded scripts at about $0.015 and 5 s per plan. Haiku 5.5 was $0.001 per plan but overshot the word target on 3 of 5 apps and chose weaker actions (it clicked "Log in"). The no-AI fallback was clearly worse than all three. Rerun with `npm run bakeoff` after prompt changes.
- Server-side refusal fallback is on for Sonnet and Opus (`fallbacks: "default"`, beta `server-side-fallback-2026-07-01`): a cyber or frontier_llm refusal is retried on Sonnet 5 inside the same call. Cost is summed over `usage.iterations`, since top-level `usage` only covers the attempt that answered. If the whole chain refuses, the no-AI fallback planner takes over.
- Structured output via `output_config.format` with `PLAN_JSON_SCHEMA` (built from the zod schema). Do not switch to the SDK's `zodOutputFormat` helper: it turns `enum` into a description hint.
- Exactly **one** planning call per trailer. Never a screenshot-act-screenshot agent loop.
- Check `stop_reason`. On `refusal`, `max_tokens` or invalid JSON, use the fallback planner.
- Plans are cached per normalized URL, model, style, running time and director's notes (a hash of them), tied to the capture they were made from (element ids only hold for that capture), so re-rendering the same app costs nothing. Demo-link jobs are never cached, since their capture is private.
- `validatePlan` rejects a plan under 40 or over 170 words (`WORDS_HARD`; 25 to 110 at 30 s and 12 to 55 at 15 s in `WORDS_HARD_BY_LENGTH`, whose ceilings also cap a recut's script). The floor has to sit below every style's target: at 60 it silently threw away good 55 to 59 word hype scripts (Fundable's and vibestarter.xyz's) for the no-AI planner, and the issue list never said why.
- Prompt version 7. Version 5 added the style section: hype asks for 5 or 6 shots, lines of 5 to 10 words, about 70 words and at most 85, because every shot costs a punch title (a 7-shot, 76-word hype plan read by Thalia ran 53.8 s under version 4). Version 6 added the 30 and 15 second cuts and fuller walkthroughs (6 or 7 shots, lines of 12 to 16 words, 100 to 125 words; under version 5 Opus wrote about 90 and they cut to 33 s). Version 7 added director's notes. It also asks for `publicSafe`, the gallery judgment. Every shot gets a punch title; plans without one (fallback, older saved plans) get one from the page in `validatePlan`. The JSON schema strips `default` so the model always writes the field. Version 2 let Sonnet run to about 137 words (a 55 second trailer). Bump `PROMPT_VERSION` whenever the prompt or schema changes.
- `PLANNER=mock` returns saved example plans. Use it for all work on recording, voice and rendering.
- Every plan, from any planner, goes through `validatePlan`: unknown ids, external, mailto and destructive clicks, and typing into sensitive fields are dropped; em dashes are cleaned out of the copy.
- **Fallback planner (no AI):** hold on the hero, hover the primary call to action, scroll to the strongest sections (or hover the controls of a single-screen app), then click in, with a script built only from the page's own prose (description, headings, visible text). The app must keep working at $0 if the API is unavailable or the budget is hit.

### API budget

The Claude API credit is shared with another project, so Premiere must use as little as possible.

- Premiere has its own Console workspace with a small monthly spend limit, and its own API key. That limit is the hard cap.
- The key lives only in `.env` (gitignored) as `PREMIERE_ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`. It is deliberately not named `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, and the client is only built with that key passed explicitly, so it never picks up other credentials on the machine. Never export any API key in the shell: Claude Code would then bill the API key instead of the subscription.
- App-side budget: `PLANNER_MONTHLY_BUDGET_USD` (default $5, keep it at or below the Console limit). At about $0.04 per Opus plan, $5 covers roughly 120 trailers a month; raise both limits before judging week if traffic needs it. Each call's cost is computed from `usage` and recorded in a ledger; once the budget is reached, the planner switches to the fallback. The ledger is a local file for now and moves to the job database when the worker goes to the VPS.
- Free trailers have a per-visitor daily limit (5, so a judge can try several apps) and a global daily cap, and access codes can gate them as an emergency lever (`worker/src/server/access.ts`, set with `ACCESS_CODES`, `ACCESS_PER_VISITOR_DAILY` and `ACCESS_DAILY_CAP`; the form calls them access codes). The web app sends a hash of the visitor's IP; the worker stores only an HMAC of that, keyed with a random secret it creates in `.data/access-secret`, with the day's counts in `.data/access.json`. A plain hash would not do: anyone could hash every IPv4 address and reverse it. Admission is counted when a job starts, so a failed job still uses one of the visitor's free trailers. With no codes set, access is open but the limits still apply.

### Safety

The worker opens arbitrary URLs, so treat every submitted URL as hostile:
- Allow only `http` and `https`. Resolve the hostname and refuse private, loopback, link-local and cloud-metadata addresses, including after redirects.
- How: the URL is checked before launch, then every browser connection goes through a local egress proxy (`safety/egress-proxy.ts`) that resolves the host itself, refuses non-public addresses and connects to the exact IP it checked. This covers redirects, subresources, iframes and DNS rebinding. Playwright's `route()` is not enough on its own: redirect hops skip it.
- No downloads, no file access, no extra permissions. Fresh browser context per job.
- Hard limits on page load time, total job time and output size.

## Website design: the night-shift drive-in

The site is a drive-in theater for apps, open all night (the Vibeathon theme). Keep new pages inside this world rather than adding generic SaaS sections. Every element should be a real object from a drive-in (a sign, a ticket, a handbill, a slide, a can, a board), not a themed card. The first version read as AI slop because every section repeated one formula: a tiny mono label, a serif headline with one italic amber word, and a grid of rounded glowing cards. Do not bring that back.

- **Hero:** a starry sky (no moon here; it sits behind the sign), a full-width roadside marquee whose crooked black changeable letters carry the headline and whose bottom row spells the typed app name, the box office ticket (the order form) on the left, the big screen on posts playing a real Premiere trailer (Squoosh's) on the right, muted with a sound button, under a caption naming the app, and the lot: 1950s cars as silhouettes rimmed with screen light. Mouse parallax moves stars, hills and cars by different amounts. On phones the screen comes straight after the marquee, before the ticket, and the projector beam is hidden (it only lines up with the cars on wide screens).
- **The ticket** (`components/hero/UrlForm.tsx`, `pickers.tsx`): ADMIT ONE on paper stock with a perforated edge and a red serial. Tick boxes for the cut, a car radio for the narrator (drive-ins played the soundtrack over FM; each voice is a station with a sample to hear), little frames for the caption looks, tick boxes for the running time (Trailer, Teaser, Spot) and the screen (Wide, Tall), two `+ Add` toggles that unfold a typed field for director's notes and one for a signed-in demo link (which unticks and locks the gallery box), and the gallery opt-in. The site never says "Vibeathon": it reads as a product, free until 16 Nov and then a few USDC (only code comments and these notes mention the event).
- **Sections, as the night's program:** Tonight's program (a printed handbill of five reels timed to the second like a running order, 2:00:00 to 2:01:16 a.m. and ready before 2:03, from a real job; a clock's red second hand sweeps as you scroll; keep the times honest if the pipeline speeds up or slows down), Previously on the big screen (the gallery as lobby cards), Intermission (a projected teal slide: why it is called a trailer, ads spliced onto the end of the reel in 1913; say "one of the earliest", not "the first"), Four cans go home with you (steel film cans with masking-tape labels in marker), Box office (a felt letter board, plus a typed card for agents with the x402 request), Last show, then rolling end credits.
- **Screening room** (`/jobs/[id]`): the same sky, a film-strip progress view of the five steps (a queued job shows roughly when it starts), the trailer with download tickets and two share tickets (Post on X; Copy link, which falls back to a hidden text field where the clipboard API is refused, and opens the share sheet on phones), then the storyboard, the script and the cutting room: "Mark up the script" opens the script as revised pages on paper stock (each line in a typed field, hype punch titles in League Gothic), with the ticket's car radio and caption frames and a red RECUT IT button that stays off until something changes. A recut's page links back to the first take. The gallery page is `/gallery`.
- **Link previews:** every trailer page unfurls with its thumbnail (and `og:video`), using absolute URLs from `SITE_URL` or the request's host (`app/jobs/[id]/layout.tsx`); the site itself uses a 1200×630 shot of the hero (`app/opengraph-image.png`, redo it if the hero changes). The favicon is the wordmark's neon P on a night-ink tile (`app/favicon.ico`, `icon.png`, `apple-icon.png`), rendered from the real Yellowtail.
- **Type:** League Gothic (the condensed poster gothic on old movie posters and marquees) for headlines, prices and signs, set in capitals; Yellowtail (a sign painter's script) for the neon wordmark and a few lit signs; Libre Franklin (a Franklin Gothic revival) for reading; Courier Prime for anything typed (labels, times, the screenplay); Permanent Marker only for labels written on tape. No italics as decoration.
- **Look:** night ink `#07080c`, cream `#f3ead8`, incandescent amber `#ffb547`, ticket red `#c8372d`, paper `#efe3c6` with dark ink `#241a10`, slide teal `#0f4a4f`, and element-label pink `#e11d74` for the storyboard only. Hero entrances are CSS (`.rise`), so the page reads before it hydrates, and they wait for the film leader. Every animation has a `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback.
- **Grain stays behind text.** A static grain is baked into the page and sky backgrounds. Never put an overlay (grain, noise, vignette) above the content: an animated grain layer on top made all text look blurry, especially on scaled Windows displays.
